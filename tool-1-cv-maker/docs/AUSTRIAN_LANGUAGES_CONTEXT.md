# Austrian Language Context: Why These 14 Languages?

## Austria's Linguistic Landscape

Austria's population is **9.4 million** (2025), with approximately **17-20% foreign-born residents**. This document explains why the CV Maker supports exactly these 14 languages.

---

## The 14 Core Languages Explained

### DACH Region (4 languages)

**Germany-Austria-Switzerland borders** - Natural language communities

#### German (de) - PRIMARY
- **Speaker Base**: 8.9 million native speakers in Austria
- **Role**: Official language, AMS headquarters language
- **Rationale**: Foundational language for all Austrian employment
- **Output Priority**: PRIMARY (all CVs default to German)

#### English (en) - SECONDARY  
- **Speaker Base**: ~2-3 million English speakers in Austria
- **Role**: International standard, tech industry lingua franca
- **Rationale**: Global employment, tech sector, EU compliance
- **Output Priority**: SECONDARY (all CVs include English)

#### Italian (it) - Regional
- **Speaker Base**: ~20,000 native speakers (South Tyrol border region)
- **Region Concentration**: Tyrol/Vorarlberg border, South Tyrolean diaspora
- **Rationale**: Immediate geographic neighbor, South Tyrol shared culture
- **Use Case**: Cross-border workers, regional cooperation

#### French (fr) - Western Neighbor
- **Speaker Base**: ~10,000 native speakers in Austria
- **Region**: Western Austria, EU context
- **Rationale**: EU member state, Western European integration
- **Use Case**: International EU roles, diplomatic/org positions

---

### Central European Neighbors (4 languages)

**Immediate geographic and economic neighbors** - Historical migration patterns

#### Polish (pl)
- **Speaker Base**: ~60,000-70,000 Poles in Austria (~0.7% population)
- **Immigration Pattern**: Largest single immigrant group after Turks/ex-Yugoslavs
- **Rationale**: Major Central European neighbor, significant workforce
- **Use Case**: Manual labor, construction, healthcare, domestic work

#### Czech (cs)
- **Speaker Base**: ~30,000-40,000 Czechs in Austria
- **Region**: Bordering Bohemia, historical Vienna-Prague corridor
- **Rationale**: Direct neighbor, regional labor market integration
- **Use Case**: Technical roles, craftspeople, management

#### Slovak (sk)
- **Speaker Base**: ~20,000-25,000 Slovaks in Austria
- **Region**: Northeast border, commuter population
- **Rationale**: Direct neighbor, integrated labor market
- **Use Case**: Technical trades, services, healthcare

#### Hungarian (hu)
- **Speaker Base**: ~30,000-35,000 Hungarians in Austria
- **Region**: East border, Budapest-Vienna corridor
- **Rationale**: Historical region (Austro-Hungarian Empire), direct neighbor
- **Use Case**: Professional roles, technical expertise, management

---

### Eastern European Slavic (4 languages) - NEW

**Post-1990s migration, refugee support, workforce**

#### Ukrainian (uk) - NEW: HUMANITARIAN PRIORITY
- **Speaker Base**: ~60,000+ Ukrainians (as of 2024-2025, rapid growth)
- **Status**: REFUGEE POPULATION (2022-2025 refugee crisis)
- **Rationale**: 
  - Fastest-growing minority (2023-2025)
  - Humanitarian priority (EU position)
  - Integration support for displaced workers
  - Future economic contributions
- **Use Case**: All sectors, refugee re-employment, integration support
- **Timeline**: Added to core May 2026 due to humanitarian need

#### Russian (ru) - SIGNIFICANT POPULATION
- **Speaker Base**: ~45,000-60,000 Russians/Russian speakers in Austria
- **Immigration Pattern**: Post-Soviet era, tech professionals, oligarch wealth
- **Rationale**:
  - Significant existing population
  - Professional/technical roles
  - Business/diplomatic community
- **Use Case**: Tech roles, professional services, finance
- **Note**: Business/political complications, but humanitarian priority for workers

#### Serbian (sr) - FORMER YUGOSLAV DIASPORA
- **Speaker Base**: ~100,000+ (single largest non-EU immigrant group)
- **Immigration Pattern**: 1990s Balkan Wars exodus, established community
- **Concentration**: Vienna, Graz, industrial centers
- **Rationale**:
  - Largest immigrant community (1% of Austrian population)
  - Well-established second/third generation
  - Strong labor market participation
  - Construction, manual labor, services
- **Use Case**: Entire economy, all sectors

#### Bosnian (bs) - FORMER YUGOSLAV DIASPORA
- **Speaker Base**: ~50,000-60,000 Bosnians in Austria
- **Immigration Pattern**: 1990s Bosnian War refugees, family reunification
- **Concentration**: Vienna, Linz, Graz
- **Rationale**:
  - Second-largest ex-Yugoslav group
  - Well-integrated, diverse employment
  - Multi-generational presence
- **Use Case**: All sectors, integrated workforce

---

### Turkic Language Family (1 language)

#### Turkish (tr) - MAJOR IMMIGRANT GROUP
- **Speaker Base**: ~50,000 Turks + heritage speakers (~0.5% population)
- **Immigration Pattern**: Guest worker program (1960s-1970s), family reunification
- **Concentration**: Vienna, Linz, Graz, all major cities
- **Rationale**:
  - Historic "Gastarbeiter" (guest worker) program
  - Multi-generational communities
  - Significant business/entrepreneurship
  - Well-integrated despite integration challenges
- **Use Case**: All sectors, strong entrepreneurship, services, construction
- **Note**: Often overlooked but crucial for Austrian economy

---

### Semitic Language (1 language)

#### Arabic (ar) - SYRIAN DIASPORA + MENA
- **Speaker Base**: ~40,000-50,000 Arabic speakers
  - ~30,000 Syrians (2015-2023 refugee crisis)
  - ~10,000 other MENA speakers (Palestinians, Egyptians, etc.)
- **Immigration Pattern**: Syrian War refugee crisis (2015 onwards)
- **Concentration**: Vienna (~60% of Syrian refugees), Graz, Linz
- **Rationale**:
  - Second-largest refugee population (after Ukraine 2024)
  - Growing integration, professional skills
  - Healthcare workers, engineers, academics
  - Future economic potential
- **Use Case**: Healthcare, technical roles, education, services
- **Timeline**: Significant since 2015, continuing (albeit below peak 2015-2017)

## Design Decision: 14 Core Languages

### Selection Criteria

1. **Geographic Proximity**
   - DACH neighbors (4): German, English, Italian, French
   - Direct borders (4): Polish, Czech, Slovak, Hungarian

2. **Migration Volume**
   - Threshold: >20,000 speakers in Austria
   - Yugoslav diaspora (2): Serbian, Bosnian
   - Turkish guest workers (1): Turkish
   - Syrian refugees (1): Arabic
   - Eastern European migration (1): Ukrainian

3. **Economic Importance**
   - Labor force participation
   - Construction, healthcare, services sectors
   - Professional/technical roles

4. **Humanitarian Priority**
   - Ukrainian refugees (2022-2025)
   - Syrian refugees (2015-2025)
   - Displaced persons support

5. **Historical Context**
   - Austro-Hungarian Empire remnants (Hungarian, Czech, Polish)
   - Post-WWII migration (Italian, Yugoslav)
   - Post-Cold War (Polish, Czech, Slovak, Russian, Ukrainian)
   - Post-Yugoslav Wars (Serbian, Bosnian)
   - Post-Syrian War (Arabic)

### Expansion Potential

**Optional Languages** (on-demand, 12 total):
- Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese (Simplified/Traditional)
- Scandinavian: Swedish, Danish, Norwegian
- European: Greek, Dutch
- Asian: Japanese, Korean
- Others: Can be added as user demand grows

## Accessibility & Inclusion

### Who Benefits Most

1. **Refugees/Displaced Persons**
   - Ukrainian refugees (2022-2025): ~65,000 in Austria
   - Syrian refugees (2015-2025): ~50,000 in Austria
   - Other humanitarian cases

2. **Economic Migrants**
   - Guest worker descendants (Turkish, Yugoslav)
   - EU mobile workers (Polish, Czech, Slovak, Hungarian)
   - Professional migrants (Russian, German professionals)

3. **Second/Third Generation**
   - Children of immigrants
   - Multilingual capabilities
   - Integration support

4. **Language Communities**
   - Elderly immigrants (native language preferred)
   - Low-German-proficiency workers
   - Non-native German speakers

## Conclusion

The 14 core languages represent:
- **Geographical reality**: Neighbors + EU integration
- **Demographic reality**: Actual immigrant populations in Austria
- **Economic reality**: Workforce participation patterns
- **Humanitarian reality**: Refugee and displaced person support
- **Practical reality**: Where AMS participants live and work

This implementation reflects Austria's multicultural reality while maintaining focus on the most impactful language communities for employment support.
